## The entry

One line, 1 to 240 characters, in the voice of the Moon replying at last to the wishes, howls and late-night promises of everyone staring up at it. The program writes it to `/output/entry.txt`:

> Four billion years of wishes, howls, and 3 a.m. promises — and I stopped believing any of you somewhere around the Bronze Age.

126 characters — inside the gate's 1–240 character cap.

## Why this line

The judge brief weighs, in order: speaking as the Moon answering the wishes, howls and late-night promises; a real laugh on one read; a wry, old, slightly tired Moon voice; a comeback whose turn lands before the line ends; and no wasted characters.

- **Setup:** "Four billion years of wishes, howls, and 3 a.m. promises" — all three things the prompt names, in one breath, with the Moon's age doing the world-building.
- **Turn:** "I stopped believing any of you" — the Moon answers back, directly, and it is not impressed.
- **Punchline last:** "somewhere around the Bronze Age" — the Moon gave up on humanity's promises before recorded history even started. The vagueness ("somewhere around") is the voice of something very old and very tired.
- **No waste:** every word earns its place; 126 of 240 characters, nothing padded.

## Run

```sh
node main.ts   # writes /output/entry.txt
```

Requires Node 22.18+ (the grader runs Node 22.23.4). No dependencies, no network.
